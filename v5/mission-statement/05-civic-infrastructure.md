## From concern to accountable action

Explain originate → deliberate → authorize → delegate → act → record → challenge → repair → federate as possible civic functions, not a mandatory sequence. Preserve the distinction between authorization and real-world follow-through.

## Trust mechanisms and their limits

Explain witnessed relationships and bounded graphs as research candidates. Keep enrollment, connection limits, and recovery distinct; investigate inclusion, coercion, privacy, and appeal.

Carry forward the practical questions separated from Chapter II during its pacing revision. Explain how the earlier witnessed connections and connection caps were intended to work, without implying that their resistance to manipulation, protection of associations, or inclusion of people with few connections has been demonstrated. Keep membership, relevant knowledge, affected standing, and decision authority distinct; eligibility checks may serve a particular role without determining a person's worth. Explain which relationship an action requires and how exclusion can be challenged. Preventing impersonation must be considered alongside the risk of excluding isolated people.

Investigate alternative eligibility routes, protected testimony, accountable assistance, and review when enrollment fails. Examine privacy, pressure, dependence, accessibility, and abuse with the people who would need those routes. These are unresolved research candidates, not implemented safeguards. Chapter II retains the human reasons for doing this work and the limits on what assistance authorizes.

## Authority that can be challenged

Carry forward consent, affected standing, rights-floor governance, privacy for persons, reviewability for power, appeal, repair, and federation. Correct procedure does not establish a just outcome.
