# AUU funding and organization options in British Columbia

**Decision memo — 31 August 2026**

This is a practical planning memo, not legal, tax, accounting, or benefits advice. AUU should obtain advice based on its actual facts before accepting public money.

## Bottom line

Do not run a meaningful AUU fundraiser into a personal bank account. A crowdfunding platform does not turn project revenue into a non-taxable personal gift, and it does not keep project money separate from personal income or assets.

The best long-term fit for AUU's present public-interest and non-owner-governed posture is likely an ordinary B.C. society, provided at least three reliable directors are ready to share legal responsibility and accept real board governance. If AUU is not ready for that, use a compatible fiscal host as a bridge. The host must legally receive and control the money under a written agreement; it is more than a payment processor.

The key distinction is ownership:

- Money received and controlled by a properly operated society belongs to the society and is reported in its books and filings. It is not automatically the founder's personal income.
- Salary, contractor fees, honoraria, taxable benefits, or other compensation paid to the founder are personal income and may affect tax and disability assistance.
- Properly documented reimbursement of genuine project expenses is different from compensation, but the policy and records must be clean and its treatment should be confirmed in advance.
- Money deposited into a personal account or raised in a personal capacity creates much greater tax, benefits, ownership, and accounting uncertainty, even if the organizer intends to spend it on AUU later.

If AUU somehow raised $5 million through a society, the society would report and account for the $5 million. The founder would personally report what the society actually paid or made available to the founder as compensation or taxable benefits—not the organization's entire fundraising total. At that scale, audited or professionally reviewed financial controls, legal counsel, a CPA, an independent board, insurance, and sophisticated reporting would be essential.

## Structure comparison

| Structure | What happens to campaign funds | Advantages | Main problems | Fit for AUU now |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Personal fundraiser or sole proprietorship | Funds flow to the founder or the founder's business. | Fast and simple to start. | Personal tax and PWD ambiguity, commingling, personal control, weaker accountability, and possible personal liability. | Poor fit for meaningful public funding. |
| Fiscal host or sponsor | An existing organization receives and administers funds under an agreement. | Faster than creating a full organization; separate custody, bookkeeping, and established controls may be available. | Host fees, narrower autonomy, acceptance criteria, and dependence on the agreement. Canadian and project fit must be vetted. | Best bridge if AUU is not ready for a board. |
| B.C. society | The incorporated society owns funds and contracts, keeps books, and acts through its directors and bylaws. | Separate legal capacity, limited member liability, public-interest governance, and a durable home for grants and donations. | Requires at least three directors, governance, annual filings, tax filings, records, conflict controls, and genuine separation from the founder. | Likely best long-term fit. |
| For-profit corporation | The corporation owns funds; investors or shareholders own economic rights. | Appropriate for commercial investment, equity, and profit distribution. | Investor ownership and returns can conflict with AUU's anti-capture and public-interest posture. | Use only if AUU intentionally becomes an investor-owned business. |
| Registered charity | A qualifying charitable organization can issue official donation receipts and is subject to charity regulation. | Tax-receipted gifts and access to some charitable funding. | Registration is demanding; purposes and activities must fit charity law, with continuing compliance and restrictions. | Possible later path, not the first fundraising prerequisite. |

## Why a B.C. society is the likely destination

Under the [B.C. Societies Act](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/15018_01), a society has the capacity and powers of an individual, its members are not liable for its obligations merely because they are members, and it generally may use money and property only for its purposes rather than distribute profits to members. A society needs at least three directors, and at least one must ordinarily reside in British Columbia. A majority of directors of an ordinary society must not be paid under employment or service contracts. Director remuneration must be permitted by the bylaws; reasonable expenses may be reimbursed, subject to the bylaws.

Current provincial filing fees list basic society incorporation at C$100 and name approval at C$30. The C$130 filing cost is minor; the real cost is responsible governance, bookkeeping, annual filings, insurance, professional advice, and the time of directors. See the [B.C. Corporate Registry fee schedule](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/business/managing-a-business/permits-licences/businesses-incorporated-companies/forms-corporate-registry) and the province's [not-for-profit organization guidance](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/non-profits-sector/not-for-profit-organizations).

Incorporating a society does not automatically make AUU a registered charity. The Canada Revenue Agency distinguishes a non-profit organization from a registered charity: an NPO may operate for civic improvement or social welfare and generally cannot make its income available for members' personal benefit, but it cannot issue official donation receipts. A registered charity or other qualified donee can issue official receipts. See the CRA's [charity and NPO comparison](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/charities-giving/about-registered-charities/what-difference-between-a-registered-charity-a-non-profit-organization.html).

An incorporated NPO generally files a T2 corporate income tax return even when no tax is payable. It may also need a T1044 NPO information return if, among other triggers, relevant investment income exceeds C$10,000 or total assets exceeded C$200,000 at the end of the preceding fiscal period; once required, that filing continues in later periods. Confirm the exact obligations with a CPA using the CRA's [T4117 NPO information-return guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4117/income-tax-guide-non-profit-organization-information-return.html).

## Why “donation” on a campaign page is not enough

The CRA says a bona fide gift is a voluntary transfer for which the donor receives no advantage. Its guidance also identifies organized solicitation, recurrence, an expectation of payment, and a connection to a business, profession, service, or occupation as factors that can weigh against treating a voluntary payment as a non-taxable personal gift. Money received in the course of a business or profession can be taxable even when the payer calls it a gift. See the CRA's [Income Tax Folio S3-F9-C1](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/technical-information/income-tax/income-tax-folios-index/series-3-property-investments-savings-plans/series-3-property-investments-savings-plans-folio-9-miscellaneous-payments-receipts/income-tax-folio-s3-f9-c1-lottery-winnings-miscellaneous-receipts-income-losses-crime.html).

GoFundMe permits personal or business fundraisers to transfer to a personal or organization bank account, but that platform choice does not decide the Canadian tax result. GoFundMe itself tells organizers to keep records and consult a tax professional; the absence of a platform tax form is not a CRA ruling. See GoFundMe's pages on [choosing a fundraiser type](https://support.gofundme.com/hc/en-us/articles/203603984-Choosing-a-fundraiser-type) and [taxes for organizers](https://support.gofundme.com/hc/en-us/articles/204295498-Taxes-for-GoFundMe-organizers). Its current Canadian pricing page lists a transaction fee of 2.9% plus C$0.30 per contribution for individual and business fundraisers: [GoFundMe pricing](https://www.gofundme.com/c/pricing).

Use GoFundMe, if at all, after deciding who the legal recipient is. It may be a suitable public checkout page for a concrete milestone, but it is not the project structure.

## Disability-assistance implications

B.C.'s current income policy says gifts are exempt for a family unit receiving disability assistance. That does not safely answer the AUU question: organized project crowdfunding may be classified as business, self-employment, or other project income rather than a personal gift, depending on the facts. See B.C.'s [income treatment and exemptions policy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/policies-for-government/bcea-policy-and-procedure-manual/eligibility/income-treatment-and-exemptions).

For 2026, B.C. lists a C$16,200 annual earnings exemption for a single person receiving PWD assistance; different family configurations have different limits. The current single-person asset limit is C$100,000, with separate rules and possible exemptions for some business or self-employment assets. See the province's [annual earnings exemption](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/family-social-supports/services-for-people-with-disabilities/disability-assistance/on-disability-assistance/annual-earnings-exemption) and [asset limits table](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/governments/policies-for-government/bcea-policy-and-procedure-manual/bc-employment-and-assistance-rate-tables/asset-limits-table).

The safest operating assumptions are:

1. AUU project funds should be legally owned and held by the society or fiscal host, not by the founder.
2. The founder should not use an AUU account as a personal account or pay personal costs from it.
3. Any founder salary, contractor payment, honorarium, or taxable benefit must be treated as personal income and checked against current PWD rules.
4. Expense reimbursement should require receipts, a stated project purpose, approval by someone without the conflict, and records showing that it is not disguised compensation.
5. Obtain written or case-specific guidance before the fundraiser, not after the first large transfer.

A separate entity is a legitimate allocation of ownership and responsibility; it is not a device for hiding personal income. The board must control organizational money in fact, not only on paper.

## Minimum controls before accepting money

Whether AUU uses a society or fiscal host, do not launch until these are in place:

- a written agreement or constitutional documents identifying the legal receiver;
- a bank or platform account in the receiver's legal name;
- a board or host-approved milestone and line-item budget;
- at least two-person authorization or equivalent independent review for material payments;
- a chart of accounts and bookkeeping system from the first dollar;
- written expense, reimbursement, compensation, conflict-of-interest, restricted-fund, and record-retention rules;
- a rule that the founder cannot approve a payment to the founder;
- receipts, invoices, contracts, and decision records linked to every material payment;
- a privacy rule for donor and participant information;
- public explanations for campaign shortfall, excess, delay, cancellation, refund, and project-closure scenarios;
- a schedule for campaign and financial reporting; and
- confirmed tax, PWD, and filing treatment from appropriate advisers.

If compensation for the founder is part of the work, disclose it plainly in the campaign budget and have disinterested directors or the fiscal host approve it. Hidden compensation is much more dangerous than reasonable, documented, openly governed compensation.

## Recommended decision path

### Path A — form a B.C. society

Choose this path if Joshua can recruit at least two additional directors who understand that they have real duties, will challenge decisions when necessary, and are willing to share responsibility for records, money, conflicts, and the society's purposes.

Before incorporation:

1. Agree on narrow initial purposes that cover public-interest civic research, education, open documentation, and testing without promising a production government or platform.
2. Decide whether the founder may be compensated and draft bylaws and conflicts procedures accordingly.
3. Define which decisions belong to the board, which may be delegated, and which require independent approval.
4. Get legal review of purposes, bylaws, intellectual-property licensing, contributor terms, liability, and the relationship between the society and the existing repository.
5. Ask a CPA to establish the fiscal year, bookkeeping, tax-return obligations, sales-tax questions, payroll or contractor handling, and campaign records.
6. Confirm the founder's expected compensation and reimbursements with a disability-benefits specialist before payments begin.

### Path B — use a compatible fiscal host

Choose this path if the public review should proceed now but AUU is not ready for three directors and continuing administration. The host must be willing to support this type of civic research and open documentation in Canada.

The written agreement should answer:

- who legally owns donated funds and project assets;
- which AUU purposes and expenses the host permits;
- who approves spending and contracts;
- host fees and payment-processing fees;
- whether the host can employ or contract with the founder;
- how intellectual property, donor data, and public reporting are handled;
- whether tax receipts are available and for which payments;
- what happens to restricted or unused funds; and
- how AUU can leave the host or transfer a project to a later society.

Do not describe a payment as tax-deductible unless the actual receiver is legally able to issue an official receipt for that payment.

## Questions to take to advisers

### For a CPA

- If AUU incorporates as a B.C. society and seeks NPO treatment, which T2, T1044, GST/HST, payroll, contractor, and provincial filings will apply?
- How should restricted campaign funds, deferred amounts, platform fees, refunds, donated services, and foreign contributions be booked?
- Under which facts would a personal crowdfunding receipt be a gift, business income, or another receipt?
- How should founder compensation and genuine expense reimbursements be documented?
- At what funding level should AUU obtain a review engagement or audit even if not legally mandatory?

### For a lawyer

- Are the proposed society purposes compatible with an ordinary NPO and a possible later charity application?
- What governance and conflict rules are needed if the founder is also a director, employee, contractor, copyright holder, or public spokesperson?
- Who owns or licenses the existing documents, diagrams, name, domain, software, and future contributions?
- What statements can the fundraising page safely make about public benefit, outcomes, refunds, and tax receipts?
- What liability, privacy, employment, volunteer, research-participant, and insurance issues arise in Phase A?

### For a B.C. disability-assistance specialist

- How would salary, contractor income, honoraria, platform transfers, director payments, expense reimbursements, and in-kind benefits from the society or host be treated?
- What must be reported, when, and with which records?
- Would any founder-controlled account, repayable advance, trust arrangement, or organizational benefit count as a personal asset or income?
- How do the rules change if the founder's family-unit status changes?
- Can the expected treatment be confirmed in writing before the campaign launches?

## The next five actions

1. Continue the no-money external review campaign using the repository review guide.
2. Decide whether two suitable additional directors are available; if not, search for and interview compatible fiscal hosts.
3. Prepare a one-page description of AUU, the first funded milestone, expected founder role, and every contemplated payment type.
4. Take that exact fact pattern to a B.C. nonprofit lawyer, an NPO-experienced CPA, and a disability-assistance specialist.
5. Establish the legal receiver, account, board or host approvals, books, policies, and costed budget before publishing a donation link.

The goal is not to avoid reporting money. It is to make the report truthful: project funds belong to and are reported by the project organization; personal compensation belongs to and is reported by the person.
