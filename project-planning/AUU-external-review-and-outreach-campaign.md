# AUU external review and outreach campaign

**Working plan — 31 August 2026**

## Recommendation

Launch a public review campaign before launching a broad fundraising campaign. The campaign should ask people to test AUU, not endorse it. Its central invitation is:

> **Help test A Universal Union before it is built.**
>
> AUU is a proposal for shared civic infrastructure. No production system exists. Before anyone builds it, we want ordinary readers, critics, researchers, practitioners, and affected people to find where the documents and diagrams are unclear, unsafe, naive, contradictory, inaccessible, unsupported, or impossible. Choose a quick diagram check, a first-read review, or a focused review in an area you know. Agreement is not required.

This is more credible than generic advertising because it offers a concrete action, welcomes criticism, and creates public evidence about whether the proposal is understandable and worth advancing. Fundraising can follow once AUU has an accountable recipient, a costed milestone, and initial independent findings.

## What the campaign must accomplish

The campaign has four jobs, in order:

1. Test whether first-time readers can understand the proposal and its diagrams.
2. Find contradictions, harms, accessibility barriers, evidence gaps, and false assurances.
3. Recruit sustained contributors and qualified reviewers for the highest-risk questions.
4. Demonstrate enough credible interest to fund one bounded research milestone.

Follower counts, impressions, and broad expressions of support are secondary. The useful outputs are completed reviews, actionable findings, relevant contributors, documented changes, and an honest account of unresolved objections.

## The participation ladder

Do not ask a new visitor to read the entire document set. Give each person one clear next action.

| Commitment | Invitation | Expected output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quick check | Open one diagram and report what it appears to say and where it becomes hard to follow. | A diagram finding with device and viewing context. |
| First read | Read the Reader's Map and Mission Statement, then explain AUU in the reader's own words. | A comprehension report and one important question or objection. |
| Focused review | Read one document aligned with the person's experience or expertise. | A bounded issue: claim, risk, omission, contradiction, or research need. |
| Whole-set review | Reconstruct the model across the numbered documents, glossary, and diagrams. | A cross-document critique that identifies drift, lost distinctions, and unsafe assumptions. |
| Ongoing contribution | Help investigate, test, revise, facilitate, or maintain one defined workstream. | A reviewable contribution with a named owner, boundary, and result. |

The repository's `REVIEW.md` is the canonical instruction page. Every public post should link to it rather than restating a different review process.

## Audiences and the right ask

| Audience | Why they matter | First ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary critical readers | They reveal whether the core explanation works without specialist knowledge. | Reader's Map teach-back or one diagram check. |
| Disabled people, accessibility practitioners, plain-language reviewers, and translators | They can expose cognitive, sensory, language, device, time, and assisted-participation barriers. | Review one diagram or the User Experience Requirements under a stated access condition. |
| Privacy and security practitioners | They can challenge identity, recovery, evidence, visibility, abuse, and threat-boundary assumptions. | Identify one missing threat case or unjustified assurance in the Feature Sets. |
| Governance, rights, legal, and public-administration reviewers | They can test standing, authority, representation, rights floors, appeal, institutional recognition, and capture. | Review one authority or remedy path and state what would make it legitimate or invalid. |
| Civic-technology and open-source practitioners | They can test reuse claims, implementation order, interoperability, and maintenance burdens. | Review one dependency or tool comparison without starting a full build. |
| Community organizers and people affected by institutional failure | They can reveal absent actors, unsafe participation assumptions, and gaps between formal process and lived power. | Describe one scenario the model would mishandle or fail to reach. |

Specialist review is real labor. Initial bounded comments may be volunteered, but the funding plan should pay for substantial professional review and compensate research participants wherever feasible. Do not build the campaign on an expectation of unlimited free expertise.

## Campaign sequence

### Gate 0 — make the review candidate coherent

Publish the current Reader's Map, mission, numbered documents, glossary, four diagrams, contribution guide, external review guide, and review issue template. Label them as concept material, preserve the limits on unproven claims, and remove abandoned working material. Check links, diagram rendering, terminology, and public-name choices.

**Exit condition:** a stranger can find one review route, submit a finding, and tell which prose controls when a diagram and document differ.

### Wave 1 — closed critical read

Invite a small, deliberately mixed group rather than friends who are likely to be agreeable. Include at least one first-time general reader and people able to challenge accessibility, governance or rights, and privacy or security. Ask each person to choose one route and permit them to stop when comprehension fails.

Use individualized messages. Do not ask for endorsement, public promotion, or a whole-repository review. Ask whether the person is willing to inspect one defined artifact by a reasonable date.

**Exit condition:** the project has enough independent feedback to revise the review path and public explanation, and the responses reveal whether the task is realistically scoped.

### Wave 2 — targeted public review

Publish the canonical invitation and a small set of shareable excerpts:

- the one-line civic cycle;
- the compact functional model;
- “What AUU is / what AUU is not”;
- one example of how an authority claim can be challenged; and
- a direct invitation to find a failure rather than applaud the concept.

Send tailored outreach to relevant research groups, practitioner communities, civic-technology projects, accessibility communities, community organizations, and individual writers whose existing work clearly overlaps a specific AUU question. Each message should explain why that person's lens is relevant and request one bounded review. Avoid mass unsolicited messaging.

Do not buy broad advertising yet. First learn which message causes relevant people to open the material and complete a review. If a paid test is later justified, use a small capped budget to test one audience and one call to action, then stop if it produces traffic without substantive feedback.

**Exit condition:** AUU has findings from more than one audience, can distinguish repeated comprehension failures from isolated preferences, and has identified people who may continue with a bounded workstream.

### Wave 3 — publish what the review changed

Create a short public review report containing:

- who was invited and which perspectives remain absent, without exposing private identities;
- how many reviews were completed by route;
- the most important confirmed strengths, failures, risks, and disagreements;
- what changed in the documents;
- what was deferred or rejected and why;
- unresolved questions that require paid specialist work; and
- the next stop/go decision.

This report is the bridge from attention to trust. It should show that criticism changes the project and that disagreement is not erased.

### Wave 4 — fund one bounded milestone

Only after the legal receiver and budget controls are established, invite funding for a named Phase A outcome. A suitable first milestone is:

> **Independent review and the AUU research constitution:** define the rights floor, authority and appeal model, claims register, privacy and threat-review requirements, accessibility test plan, governance safeguards, and the evidence gates that must be passed before prototyping.

The fundraising page should not imply that the platform will be built from this first round. It should name concrete outputs, reviewers or selection processes, completion criteria, a schedule range, and what would cause the work to stop.

## Public message system

### Campaign title

**Help Test A Universal Union Before It Is Built**

### Short description

AUU is an open proposal for civic tools that connect public concerns to deliberation, limited authority, action, records, challenge, and repair. Before anyone builds it, the project needs people to inspect the documents and diagrams for confusion, danger, contradiction, exclusion, and wishful thinking. Choose one small review route. You do not have to support the idea.

### Social post

> A Universal Union is a proposal for shared civic infrastructure—not a party, government, or finished platform. Before anyone builds it, I want people to find where it breaks.
>
> You can inspect one diagram, do a first-read teach-back, or review one document in an area you know. Criticism is the assignment; endorsement is not.
>
> [Review AUU]

### Individual outreach message

> I am seeking a bounded critical review of A Universal Union, an early proposal for civic infrastructure. Your work on **[specific subject]** appears directly relevant to **[specific AUU question or artifact]**. Would you be willing to inspect **[one diagram or section]** and identify what is unclear, unsafe, unsupported, or missing? This is not a request for endorsement, and a short response or a decision to stop where the material fails is useful. The review instructions are here: **[link]**.

### Funding transition

> The first review showed which questions cannot responsibly be answered by more drafting alone. This campaign funds a bounded independent-review and research milestone. It does not sell governance, authority, or endorsement. The budget, fund holder, payment rules, conflicts, outputs, and stop conditions are disclosed below.

## Funding-page requirements

Before the campaign accepts money, publish:

- the legal name of the organization or fiscal host receiving the funds;
- the relationship between that receiver, AUU, and the founder;
- the exact milestone and deliverables;
- a line-item budget based on quotes or defensible estimates;
- whether founder compensation is included and how it is approved;
- who may authorize spending and how conflicts are handled;
- how expenses, decisions, and progress will be reported;
- what contributors receive, if anything;
- a clear statement that donations do not purchase decision power, authority, or favorable conclusions;
- what happens if the target is missed, exceeded, delayed, or the work is stopped; and
- which amounts, if any, can be refunded and under what conditions.

A first budget should include legal and accounting setup, independent governance and rights review, privacy and security threat review, accessibility and comprehension testing with participant compensation, research and facilitation, publishing or hosting costs, payment-platform fees, contingency, and any disclosed project coordination compensation. Set the target after obtaining quotes; do not choose a dramatic round number and invent a budget afterward.

## GoFundMe decision

GoFundMe can be a payment and storytelling surface, but it cannot decide who owns the money, whether a receipt is tax-deductible, how benefits rules apply, or who has authority to spend it. It is a plausible option only after AUU has a proper receiver and a concrete milestone that a general audience can understand.

Use GoFundMe if the likely contributors are individuals who expect a simple campaign page and the legal recipient can receive its payouts. Consider a compatible fiscal host or another transparent project-funding service if itemized expenses, recurring contributors, and public bookkeeping matter more. Platform selection follows the legal and governance decision; it does not replace it.

## Measures that matter

Track a small public dashboard or review report:

| Measure | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Completed reviews by route | Shows whether the participation ladder works. |
| Actionable findings by category | Shows whether attention produces knowledge. |
| Teach-back accuracy and repeated misunderstandings | Tests the core explanation. |
| Reviewers and findings across different perspectives | Exposes blind spots and overconcentration. |
| Findings addressed, deferred, rejected, or unresolved | Tests whether the project is genuinely correctable. |
| Continuing contributors with bounded roles | Measures community formation rather than passive attention. |
| Cost per completed review for any paid experiment | Prevents impressions from masquerading as progress. |
| Funded deliverables completed against budget | Tests stewardship after fundraising begins. |

Do not publicly rank individual reviewers, turn contribution into a popularity score, or imply that the largest number of comments settles a rights or evidence question.

## Immediate operating checklist

1. Merge the review-candidate cleanup and open the review issue template.
2. Put `REVIEW.md` behind every review call to action.
3. Build a private outreach list organized by the six audience lanes above.
4. Invite the closed critical read and record where people stop.
5. Revise the explanation and review path from those results.
6. Run the targeted public review and publish a findings register.
7. In parallel, choose a B.C. society or compatible fiscal host and obtain tax, disability-assistance, and legal confirmation.
8. Get quotes for the bounded Phase A work and publish the resulting milestone budget.
9. Launch fundraising only when the receiver, controls, disclosures, and stop conditions are ready.

The rational order is therefore **review → evidence → organization → costed milestone → fundraising**, with organization work occurring in parallel early enough that credible interest does not force money into a personal account.
