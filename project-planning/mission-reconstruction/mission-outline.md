## Thesis

Proposed thesis for `v5/mission-statement/README.md`:

> A Universal Union begins with faith in humanity: human dignity, agency, conscience, and relationships are the foundations of civic life. It proposes shared civic infrastructure through which people can reason together, organize limited authority, act peacefully, learn from consequences, and correct failures across generations and communities.
>
> The ambition is to change conditions that reward manipulation and domination while protecting pluralism, dissent, and people who cannot safely participate. Human trust is an ethical starting point. The mechanisms intended to support it remain proposals to investigate, challenge, and revise.

The proposed document title is **A Universal Union: Mission Statement**. The thesis and table below are a reviewable README proposal. Their links lead to real outline headings in this planning chapter; no mission chapters have been created. At an authorized scaffold stage, replace these links with the stable chapter paths and actual subsection anchors after those files exist. The [current mission](../../v5-documents/0.mission-statement.md) remains the public text.

## I. The Human Invitation

**Proposed file:** `human-invitation.md`. **Source basis:** current mission opening; S8; inventory A1 and A6.

### What do you want to change?

Keep the current opening question, ordinary local concerns, and contrasting tax, support, and immigration perspectives. The reader should encounter a human concern before a theory of governance. Preserve the distinction between hearing a concern and accepting a harmful answer.

### From engagement to action

Keep the exchange that ends in anger, the need for change, and the zoomable diagnostic link. Retain the current plain definition of civic infrastructure early in the chapter so the reader knows what is being proposed before entering the longer argument. Develop the machinery later in Chapter V.

### Better disagreement

Preserve the current account of translation across perspectives and the trans-rights example, including the statement that basic rights do not depend on winning a debate. Empirical claims about persuasion remain subject to evidence review. Lead into the question of why human dignity and relationships are the starting commitment.

## II. Faith in Humanity

**Proposed file:** `faith-in-humanity.md`. **Source basis:** current author guidance; S0–S5 trust passages; current ethics §2.1; inventory A1–A3 and A13.

### Human trust

Develop the author's ethical commitment to people, conscience, agency, and relationships. Explain why machinery serves those purposes. Distinguish relational trust in ordinary life from the [glossary's contextual design term](../../v5-documents/GLOSSARY.md#trust) and the engineering question of whether a particular graph or credential helps. A conviction about dignity does not require a demonstration of universal human reliability.

### People beyond the graph

Address isolation, abusive relationships, unfamiliar communities, displacement, dissent, and inability to enroll. The value of relationships must not become a demand that people acquire witnesses to deserve protection or standing. Explain this commitment before Chapter V addresses the unresolved mechanics of alternative participation routes.

## III. The Civilizational Problem

**Proposed file:** `civilizational-problem.md`. **Source basis:** S0 Introduction; S5 B) Introduction; S6 foundational-purpose section; current ethics §3; inventory A4–A7.

### Power and civic capacity

Reconstruct the argument connecting technological acceleration, concentrated resources, institutional gatekeeping, and the difficulty of turning shared concern into action. Identify mechanisms and examples with explicit limits. The future manuscript needs primary evidence for specific political, economic, or historical claims; it should not inherit v4's unsupported numerical generalizations.

### The conditions we reproduce

Examine how incentives around attention, funding, institutions, and ordinary choices can reward manipulation or cooperation. Preserve individual agency and unequal responsibility. Treat the earlier psychological framing as a documented source of the argument, while following v5's focus on observable conduct and reviewable power rather than diagnostic civic rankings.

## IV. The Evolution of Collective Wisdom

**Proposed file:** `collective-wisdom.md`. **Source basis:** S5 Evolutionary Pressures on Ideologies and Bottom-Up Convergence; S6 The Long-Term Vision; current mission experiment; ethics §§2.4 and 2.7; inventory A7–A10 and A12.

### Learning across disagreement

Develop repeated interaction, reciprocal learning, institutional experimentation, and borrowing across different communities. Reuse the current zero-tax example with all its limits on authority, rights, shared obligations, nonconsent, and exported costs. Present the older convergence case as a thought experiment if retained; include failed learning, captured feedback, and stable disagreement. Neither institutional survival nor consensus establishes wisdom.

### Memory and peaceful correction

Explain what can be learned from promises, attempts, consequences, minority warnings, and revisions. Memory should make correction possible without permanently exposing people or turning mistakes into exile. Keep peaceful recourse connected to organized capacity for action; software cannot guarantee justice or that an institution responds.

## V. Civic Infrastructure

**Proposed file:** `civic-infrastructure.md`. **Source basis:** current Reader's Map, features, ethics, social structures, and glossary; inventory A2–A3 and A11–A15.

### From concern to accountable action

Explain **originate → deliberate → authorize → delegate → act → record → challenge → repair → federate**, including the fact that it is not a mandatory sequence for every interaction. Preserve the mission's path from a case to organized action and a protected record. The root retains its intersection example; this chapter can explain the cycle's rationale without reproducing every feature requirement. The root's eight diagnostic questions are a candidate for this chapter, subject to the [audit decision](root-readme-audit.md#decisions-and-comprehension-review).

### Trust mechanisms and their limits

Explain why witnessed relationships and bounded connections were proposed, distinguish enrollment, connection limits, and recovery, and name the open questions. Keep identity, recognition, reputation, eligibility, standing, and authority separate. Cite specialist research requirements instead of giving a design parameter the status of an ethical principle.

### Authority that can be challenged

Retain privacy for persons, reviewability for power, consent, affected standing, protected dissent, rights-floor governance, appeal, revocation, repair, and federation. Preserve the current rejection of a party, world government, or authority prescribing belief. Distinguish a correct process from just outcomes and a recorded mandate from real-world execution.

## VI. The Work Before Us

**Proposed file:** `work-before-us.md`. **Source basis:** S7 3. Request; current mission's closing paragraphs and Help Build It; R&D, research, review, and contribution documents; inventory A16.

### What remains to be learned

Preserve the current account of what software cannot do and what people must undertake themselves. Name uncertainty, research, reuse, accessibility, affected-person criticism, and limits on claims of technical maturity. Do not turn the ending into a build roadmap or imply that the review package proves feasibility.

### Help Build It

Preserve the existing closing invitation, including the grain-of-sand metaphor, value of skepticism, and invitation to lived experience. Retain valid links to the [review routes](../../REVIEW.md) and [contribution guide](../../CONTRIBUTING.md), where participation does not require a GitHub account. The future manuscript should make the author's responsibility and AI assistance clear without suggesting an organization already speaks unanimously.
