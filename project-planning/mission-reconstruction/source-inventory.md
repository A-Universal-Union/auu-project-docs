## Finding and scope

The short mission compresses the civilizational argument, but the current document set has not abandoned it. The [ethical discussion](../../v5-documents/1.ethical-discussion.md#3-why-civic-structure-matters) still discusses social incentives, organized power, discourse, and financial capture. Its [memory and repair argument](../../v5-documents/1.ethical-discussion.md#27-memory-repair-and-reversibility) goes beyond recording procedure. The restoration should make these reasons visible in a sustained mission while retaining their more careful treatment in v5.

The clearest gaps in the short mission are an explicit statement of faith in humanity, a developed account of relational trust, the technological/civic capacity mismatch, and the historical ambition to change conditions that reward domination. Collective learning is present, especially in the policy experiment and record-of-outcomes passages, but its broader argument is compressed. These are editorial findings from the sources below, not evidence that the proposed infrastructure will achieve its aims.

The current [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#purpose-and-intellectual-commitments) and [handoff](../CODEX-HANDOFF.md) supply the author's explicit commitment to faith in humanity. Earlier trust passages provide historical continuity; they do not establish that the same philosophical formulation appeared verbatim in every draft.

## Conflicts and editorial decisions

The recommended treatment is restoration with explicit qualification. It is not a claim that the later text is correct merely because it is later.

1. **Human relationships and measured trust.** The older documents move quickly from interpersonal trust to quantification and exclusion. The present glossary narrows the design term and features keep the graph provisional. Chapter II should state the ethical commitment; Chapter V should explain candidate mechanisms and their inclusion burden. If a distinct glossary entry for relational trust is wanted, propose it separately rather than silently changing the controlled term.
2. **Adaptation and guaranteed convergence.** v4's evolutionary section expects exploitative strategies to fail and its mock polity converges toward a hybrid. The current mission permits learning without specifying its endpoint. Recover experimentation, memory, and reciprocal correction; do not treat survival, popularity, or convergence as proof of justice. The future chapter must address capture, entrenched advantage, unsuccessful learning, and permanent disagreement.
3. **Social conditions and psychological labeling.** Preserve the argument that institutions help shape behavior. Record why the manuscript uses observable conduct rather than reproducing v4's psychological diagnoses or prevalence claims. Current ethics §3.1 already supplies that distinction. This review has not verified the clinical literature or v4's citations.
4. **Visibility and safety.** v4 expects publicly visible harmful planning alongside protected identities. v5 allows private coordination, restricted evidence, and protected review. Explain this change directly: accountability needs a justified review relationship, not universal disclosure. No privacy mechanism has been validated by this editorial exercise.
5. **Technical confidence and authority.** v4's implementation-oriented assurances and June's faster-than-government ambition are stronger than the evidence established in this repository. Preserve the goal of improving civic capacity while requiring defined comparisons and research before making performance claims. Keep the civic cycle broader than voting or ledger verification.
6. **Salvage advice and the longer mission.** S9 recommends moving several arguments away from a short mission and calls the vision too expansive for that format. The current handoff deliberately allows a longer work. Treat S9 as evidence of what was valued and redistributed, not a prohibition on recovering those arguments here.
7. **The author's voice.** The current mission uses “we” and the earlier August invitation includes first-person limitations. Preserve the source during planning. Before drafting new declarations, Joshua should decide when “I,” an invitational “we,” or “AUU proposes” is appropriate so no existing collective mandate is implied.

## What remains unverified

This inventory verifies repository provenance and compares arguments. It does not verify external research, present-day political or product claims, the effectiveness of deliberative methods, the safety of identity designs, or the feasibility of the proposed institutions. The next manuscript phase should create a claim-level evidence list for specific empirical statements. Philosophical faith in human dignity and agency is not contingent on completing that list.
