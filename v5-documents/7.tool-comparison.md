# A Universal Union: Tool and Platform Comparison

**Status:** Comparative research draft. Product and repository claims were checked against first-party sources on 27 August 2026.

## 1. Purpose

AUU is not entering an empty field. Civic platforms, group-decision tools, opinion-mapping systems, voting protocols, administrative software, DAO frameworks, finance platforms, and governance research projects each solve part of the problem.

This comparison has three jobs:

1. identify existing work AUU should use or learn from;
2. locate gaps without pretending that a proposed AUU feature is already a capability; and
3. prevent an attractive feature list from becoming a misleading product ranking.

The central finding is simple:

> Existing tools contain substantial, current knowledge. AUU's proposed distinction is the explicit connection of an entire correctable civic cycle—not superiority on every individual feature.

That distinction remains a design hypothesis until AUU builds and tests it.

## 2. How to read the comparison

### 2.1 Three questions, kept separate

For every tool, ask:

| Question | Meaning | Evidence needed |
|---|---|---|
| **Specified functional scope** | What does the project say the tool is designed to do? | Current first-party documentation and source. |
| **Demonstrated maturity** | What has actually been implemented, maintained, deployed, evaluated, and supported? | Releases, repository activity, deployments, audits, evaluations, incidents, and operational evidence. |
| **Reuse feasibility** | Can AUU lawfully and safely adopt, fork, integrate, or learn from it? | License, architecture, APIs, export, security, accessibility, maintenance, deployment, governance, and fit. |

The old comparison blurred these questions. A product can have a narrow scope and high maturity. AUU can specify a broad scope and still have no demonstrated implementation.

### 2.2 Evidence limits

The sources reviewed here are mainly maintainer documentation and public repositories. They establish what a project currently says and provide maintenance signals. They do not independently prove effectiveness, accessibility, security, adoption quality, or civic legitimacy.

A recent commit shows activity, not quality. A deployment count reported by a vendor or maintainer is not an independent evaluation. Public source does not mean every advertised feature is covered by the same license. A polished commercial service may have strong operations even when its code is not reusable.

### 2.3 Conservative symbols

The lifecycle matrix uses:

- **●** — central capability described in current first-party material;
- **◐** — supporting, configurable, or narrower capability;
- **—** — not established from the reviewed source set;
- **P** — proposed AUU requirement, not a demonstrated capability.

The symbols do not score quality or legitimacy.

## 3. The AUU benchmark

AUU's working lifecycle is:

> **originate → deliberate → authorize → delegate → act → record → challenge → repair → federate**

Across that lifecycle, AUU also proposes five requirements that ordinary feature matrices often hide:

1. **Authority provenance:** say who may bind whom, within what scope, and who recognizes that authority.
2. **Affected-person standing:** allow a person to challenge consequential power without first joining or endorsing it.
3. **Visibility discipline:** distinguish public, participant- or member-visible, protected review, and private information.
4. **Contestable interpretation:** connect summaries and analytics to sources, uncertainty, correction, and competing interpretations.
5. **Federation without absorption:** coordinate independent collectives without treating data compatibility as transferred authority.

These are comparison questions, not claims that AUU has solved them.

### 5.1 What the matrix exposes

Origination, deliberation, and voting are not empty spaces waiting for AUU. Several maintained projects already connect them. Delegation is more specialized. Finance and administrative follow-through exist in separate systems. Opinion mapping is strong but narrow. Verifiable voting is a specialist field with explicit safety limits.

The least established part of AUU's proposed cycle is not “online participation.” It is the durable connection among authority, action, public challenge, repair, and federation while maintaining protected personal information and independent standing.

That gap is a research agenda. It is not evidence that AUU is already distinct in practice.

## 6. Profiles and lessons

### 6.1 Decidim

[Decidim describes itself](https://docs.decidim.org/en/develop/features/general-description.html) as free, collaboratively built digital infrastructure for participatory democracy. Its documentation covers configurable participatory processes, assemblies and other spaces, proposals, support, comments, meetings, consultations, and institutional process design. Its public repository showed [activity on 25 August 2026](https://github.com/decidim/decidim/commit/1bf59790e1043ad0faed974ca7480263f20c86e4).

**What AUU should learn:** A civic platform becomes useful through complete, configurable processes and administrative tooling, not an abstract catalogue of democratic ideals. Decidim is also a warning against claiming “infrastructure” while leaving governance, documentation, deployment, and community stewardship unspecified.

**What AUU must not infer:** Decidim's existence does not establish AUU's personhood, privacy, standing, challenge, authority, or federation model. Nor does a missing AUU-specific concept mean Decidim lacks all related mechanisms.

**Reuse posture:** Run one AUU reference process on an unmodified Decidim deployment before deciding to fork or rebuild. Map its actors, spaces, components, records, exports, accessibility evidence, and governance to AUU's shared contracts.

### 6.2 CONSUL Democracy

[CONSUL Democracy](https://consuldemocracy.org/) describes open-source software for debates, citizen proposals, voting, participatory budgeting, and collaborative law or regulation drafting. The foundation published [version 2.5.0](https://consuldemocracy.org/2026/04/version-release-2-5-0/) in April 2026, and its public repository showed [activity on 26 August 2026](https://github.com/consuldemocracy/consuldemocracy/commit/b174353372443b514717562b3ec69f728e75fb88).

**What AUU should learn:** Government-facing workflows, moderation, administration, translation, and maintenance are core civic functions. Participatory budgeting is particularly useful because it joins proposals, feasibility, resource limits, decisions, and implementation more concretely than a generic discussion forum.

**What AUU must not infer:** A government deployment does not make every input binding or every participant represented. “Open and transparent” still requires exact visibility, data, authority, and remedy analysis.

**Reuse posture:** Compare CONSUL and Decidim using the same bounded pilot script and evidence checklist. A decision between them should depend on the pilot, stewardship, integration, and maintenance burden—not brand preference.

### 6.3 Go Vocal

[Go Vocal](https://www.govocal.com/) describes a government engagement platform with surveys, voting, mapping, budgeting, offline-input support, analysis, reporting, and project workflow. Its [development portal](https://developers.govocal.com/) states that the Open-Source or Free Edition contains core functions under AGPLv3, while advanced Commercial Edition code is source-available and requires a paid commercial license. The public repository showed [activity on 26 August 2026](https://github.com/CitizenLabDotCo/citizenlab/commit/c47d9dfa412043c59b57e222f29fd08c867ae397).

**What AUU should learn:** Outreach, multilingual work, offline-input handling, project administration, input analysis, reporting, and customer support determine whether participation can operate repeatedly. The product also shows why “source available” and “open source” must not be used interchangeably.

**What AUU must not infer:** Vendor claims, dashboards, or AI-supported analysis do not establish representative participation, neutral interpretation, authority, or challenge. The source status of one edition does not apply automatically to another.

**Reuse posture:** Treat the Free and Commercial editions as different candidates. Record the exact feature, repository, license, data export, API, hosting, model use, security, and termination terms before comparison.

### 6.4 EngagementHQ

[EngagementHQ](https://granicus.com/product/sentiment-feedback-engagementhq/) describes omnichannel government engagement using ideas, votes, questions, maps, surveys, budgets, comments, analysis, and human plus automated moderation.

**What AUU should learn:** Consultation needs recruitment, moderation, sensitivity controls, cross-channel input, analysis, and reporting to participants. A reliable operational service can be more useful for a bounded consultation than an unfinished “more democratic” platform.

**What AUU must not infer:** Feedback collection is not authorization. Sentiment classification is not a public judgment. A current commercial product is not a source-reuse candidate without a specific license or integration agreement.

**Reuse posture:** Include it in workflow and procurement benchmarks when a partner already uses it. Examine exports and APIs for connecting input to AUU-style records and challenges; do not assume direct code reuse.

### 6.5 OpenGov

[OpenGov's current platform](https://opengov.com/platform/) focuses on state and local government finance, budgeting, performance, procurement, permitting, assets, and service operations. Its [Budgeting & Performance product](https://opengov.com/products/budgeting-and-performance/) connects budget construction, publication, planning, and performance reporting.

**What AUU should learn:** Civic decisions eventually meet budgets, procurement, staff, assets, legal workflows, and performance records. Follow-through cannot be reduced to a “completed” button supplied by the decision-maker.

**What AUU must not infer:** Administrative integration is not bottom-up deliberation or independent challenge. A public dashboard can remain a curated institutional account.

**Reuse posture:** Treat OpenGov as a possible external system of action and evidence. Research authenticated imports, public links, corrections, provenance, and disagreements rather than trying to turn an administrative suite into AUU.

### 6.6 Loomio

Loomio connects threads to proposals and polls, including consent-oriented group processes and recorded reasons. Its [security and service documentation](https://help.loomio.com/en/policy/security/index.html) states that the software is AGPLv3 and can be self-hosted. Its repository showed [activity on 27 August 2026](https://github.com/loomio/loomio/commit/05af4a3852394e9216aa77fdade06a2400a78487).

**What AUU should learn:** Discussion should lead to a visible, revisable proposal; reasons should remain connected to decisions; and group facilitators need practical tools. Loomio offers a much better starting point for small-collective experiments than a new generic comment system.

**What AUU must not infer:** A well-run group process solves population eligibility, public authority, affected-nonparticipant standing, coercion, or federation.

**Reuse posture:** Test Loomio as both a hosted service and self-hosted component. Map group roles, exports, webhooks, consent flows, moderation, identity, and decision records to AUU contracts.

### 6.7 Polis

[Polis](https://pol.is/home2) describes an open-source platform for mapping opinion groups, agreement, consensus, and disagreement at large scale. Its current geo-oriented deployment describes participant comments, agree/disagree/pass voting, public data, and statistically derived collective statements. The repository showed [activity on 28 July 2026](https://github.com/compdemocracy/polis/commit/5089c6bef9eb1a1e454beb34354fb29dd0a2b6f0).

**What AUU should learn:** Large public conversations need structures other than reply trees. Cross-group agreement, disagreement, missing viewpoints, and source contributions can be made legible.

**What AUU must not infer:** A cluster is not a constituency, a consensus statement is not authorization, and a statistically central view is not necessarily just. Invitation and sampling choices affect what the map can mean.

**Reuse posture:** Use Polis as a bounded analytic component with source-linked correction and authority warnings. Compare results across seed statements, recruitment paths, moderation, model versions, and excluded participants.

### 6.8 Your Priorities

[Your Priorities](https://citizens.is/your-priorities/) describes idea generation, independent arguments for and against, voting, prioritization, surveys, and participatory budgeting. Its [open-source page](https://citizens.is/open-source/) describes MIT-licensed server and web-app packages and ongoing development; the repository showed [activity on 30 July 2026](https://github.com/CitizensFoundation/your-priorities-app/commit/5668b32fac01c9358aa93605cc967dd28f628efe).

**What AUU should learn:** Separating arguments for and against an idea can reduce the dominance of conversational combat. A modular, self-hostable engagement engine may supply more directly reusable pieces than a monolithic replacement project.

**What AUU must not infer:** Structured pro/con input eliminates trolling, captures every relevant kind of reason, or establishes the authority of a resulting priority.

**Reuse posture:** Run the same issue through Your Priorities, Decidim, CONSUL, and Loomio. Compare comprehension, inclusion, correction, data model, accessibility, operations, and export before choosing components.

### 6.9 LiquidFeedback

[LiquidFeedback's current feature material](https://liquidfeedback.com/en/full-feature-list.html) emphasizes transparent proposal development, collective moderation, minority protection, preferential voting, and transitive delegation, with configurable accreditation and organizational units.

**What AUU should learn:** Delegation can be topic-specific and revocable, and proposal development can be governed as a structured process rather than a single final ballot. The system also provides realistic cases for studying concentration and transparency tradeoffs.

**What AUU must not infer:** Public delegation and recorded votes are safe in every civic setting. Product terms such as “minority protection” or “troll resistance” need mechanism-level and field evaluation before AUU repeats them.

**Reuse posture:** Conduct a dedicated license, source, dependency, deployment, accessibility, and maintenance review before code reuse. Use a synthetic delegation lab first, with concentration, cycles, coercion, revocation, and privacy tests.

### 6.10 Helios

[Helios](https://vote.heliosvoting.org/) offers verifiable online elections and ballot trackers. Its [FAQ](https://vote.heliosvoting.org/faq) explains that the tracker supports inclusion and tally checking, while explicitly advising against high-stakes public-office Internet elections because of coercion and insecure home computers. Its repository showed [activity on 15 August 2026](https://github.com/benadida/helios-server/commit/88621e3196961ec03fe54bbd3a1c2196e715e9a2).

**What AUU should learn:** Verifiability should attach to a precise protocol claim. Just as important, a responsible tool can state where it should not be used.

**What AUU must not infer:** A ballot tracker proves eligibility, civic authority, coercion resistance, safe endpoints, or suitability for public elections. Nor should AUU copy the word “receipt” without stating whether an artifact can prove participation or choice to another person.

**Reuse posture:** Use Helios in a low-risk research lab with election and coercion expertise. Compare submission, inclusion, tally, choice-proof, and receipt-freeness properties explicitly.

### 6.11 Snapshot and Aragon

[Snapshot](https://docs.snapshot.box/) describes configurable off-chain DAO voting using spaces, proposals, voting systems, and strategies that determine voting power. [Aragon OSx](https://docs.aragon.org/) supplies permissioned, plugin-based governance and execution; its token-voting documentation makes token-proportional influence explicit, while staged proposal processing supports multiple approval or veto bodies. Aragon's public repository showed [activity on 4 August 2026](https://github.com/aragon/osx/commit/f7c7508f4ceb2b685ee037ffe87df17340fc4aef).

**What AUU should learn:** Governance modules can combine proposal rules, voting strategies, permissions, timelocks, vetoes, and executable action. Protocol state can make some forms of manipulation and unauthorized execution easier to inspect.

**What AUU must not infer:** Wallet possession, tokens, staking, reputation points, or on-chain execution establish equal civic personhood, standing, consent, or legitimate authority. Immutability can preserve error and harm as efficiently as valid action.

**Reuse posture:** Extract patterns behind narrow interfaces. Any use must replace asset-derived authority with an explicit AUU authority source unless the actual collective knowingly chooses an asset-based rule for a bounded, lawful purpose.

### 6.12 Open Collective

[Open Collective](https://opencollective.com/how-it-works) combines open-source financial tooling with fiscal hosting. A host can supply legal and financial infrastructure to an unincorporated collective, while budgets, contributions, and expenses can be made visible. Its public [frontend](https://github.com/opencollective/opencollective-frontend) and [API](https://github.com/opencollective/opencollective-api) repositories both showed activity on 25 August 2026.

**What AUU should learn:** Money has roles, approvals, legal custody, documentation, disputes, and recurring administrative work. Fiscal transparency is a concrete part of accountability.

**What AUU must not infer:** A transparent ledger proves a wise decision, proper representation, absence of conflicts, fair labor, or successful outcomes. A fiscal host's legal authority is distinct from a collective's member mandate.

**Reuse posture:** Prefer integration over rebuilding financial infrastructure. Preserve the distinction between imported transaction evidence, host assertions, collective interpretation, and independent challenge.

### 6.13 PolicyKit and Metagov Gateway

[PolicyKit](https://metagov.org/projects/policykit) is described as a way for online communities to author governance procedures and carry them out on home platforms. The [Metagov Gateway](https://github.com/metagov/gateway) connects decision tools and community platforms for governance prototyping.

**What AUU should learn:** Rules can be modeled as actions, policies, triggers, decisions, and platform adapters. This is useful for testing process logic without rebuilding every communication environment.

**What AUU must not infer:** Executable policy is correct policy. Code can conceal discretion, exclusion, unsafe defaults, or an invalid authority source behind a reproducible procedure.

**Reuse posture:** Use in the reference laboratory only after direct installation and maintenance validation. Require a plain-language rendering, authority record, test cases, change control, and challenge path for every consequential policy.

### 6.14 DemocracyOS

DemocracyOS remains a useful historical example of proposal-linked deliberation and voting. The main [public repository](https://github.com/DemocracyOS/democracyos) is not marked archived, but the latest commit observed through GitHub was [28 May 2020](https://github.com/DemocracyOS/democracyos/commit/ad8627d2b6ccfcbe41941ff6f6bb3f65dbca0a11).

**What AUU should learn:** Historical projects can clarify the lineage of civic product ideas and adoption difficulties.

**What AUU must not infer:** A reachable repository is maintained or safe to deploy. “Not archived” is not maintenance evidence.

**Reuse posture:** Treat as a source and case study, not a dependency, unless a new review establishes current stewardship, supported dependencies, security, accessibility, deployment, and a credible maintenance path.

## 7. What AUU should borrow

### 7.1 Borrow complete flows before isolated widgets

AUU should first reproduce one bounded civic question in existing tools. This reveals how origination, moderation, identity, deadlines, decisions, records, administration, and participant support interact. Copying a voting widget without its surrounding operations is likely to recreate solved problems and hide unsolved ones.

### 7.2 Borrow specialization

- Use Decidim, CONSUL, and Your Priorities to study public participation processes.
- Use Loomio to study group discussion-to-decision flows.
- Use Polis to study large-scale opinion mapping and contestable synthesis.
- Use LiquidFeedback to study delegation and structured proposal development.
- Use Helios to study precise election verifiability and explicit limits.
- Use Snapshot and Aragon to study configurable governance and executable authorization.
- Use Open Collective to study fiscal roles and transparent resource records.
- Use PolicyKit and Gateway to study policy execution across existing platforms.
- Use commercial engagement and administrative products to study operations, support, reporting, and procurement realities.

Specialization is not fragmentation if shared civic contracts preserve meaning between components.

### 7.3 Borrow honest boundaries

Helios's warning against high-stakes Internet elections and Go Vocal's distinction between AGPL core and commercially licensed advanced features are both valuable patterns. AUU should be equally explicit about immature scope, edition boundaries, unresolved tradeoffs, and uses it rejects.

## 8. What AUU must still add or test

The reviewed tools do not establish AUU's complete model. The most important open work is:

- an authority model that distinguishes preference, authorization, office, delegation, action, and recognition;
- independent standing for affected nonparticipants;
- a governed rights floor and appeal structure;
- consistent public, participant- or member-visible, protected review, and private visibility;
- source-linked, correctable, privacy-tested interpretation;
- a challenge lifecycle connected to decisions, claims, moderation, analytics, and action;
- repair that changes records, roles, mandates, and real-world follow-through where authority permits;
- safe representation of competing collectives and authority claims;
- federation with explicit recognition, dissent, conflict, exit, and migration; and
- one participant experience that makes the whole model understandable.

Some existing systems may contain partial mechanisms not visible in the sources reviewed here. AUU should test before claiming absence.

## 9. Reuse evaluation

### 9.1 Entry criteria

Before AUU adopts, forks, embeds, or depends on a tool, record:

| Area | Questions |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Which exact AUU requirement does it meet? What evidence would show it does not? |
| License | Which repository, component, edition, data, model, and documentation licenses apply? Are network-use and redistribution duties compatible? |
| Stewardship | Who governs releases, accepts security reports, resolves disputes, and can change the license or roadmap? |
| Maintenance | Are current versions supported? What is release cadence, dependency health, issue response, and fork feasibility? |
| Security | What threat model, audits, vulnerabilities, update path, isolation, and incident support exist? |
| Privacy | What data and metadata are collected, inferred, exported, retained, combined, or disclosed to operators and third parties? |
| Accessibility | What conformance, assistive-technology, cognitive, language, device, and participant evidence exists for the exact version? |
| Operations | What hosting, moderation, training, support, backups, observability, recovery, and shutdown work is required? |
| Interoperability | Are stable APIs and complete exports available? Do they preserve provenance, dissent, authority, visibility, and corrections? |
| Governance fit | Can AUU express the tool's roles and decisions without inventing legitimacy or losing affected-person standing? |
| Cost and capture | What money, staff, vendor, infrastructure, or credential dependence creates leverage? |
| Exit | Can data, records, keys, roles, and participant rights move safely if the dependency fails or changes? |

### 9.2 Reuse outcomes

The result should be one of:

- **Adopt for a bounded use:** the existing tool meets the need with documented configuration and limits.
- **Integrate as a service:** retain the tool's operation and connect through a narrow, monitored contract.
- **Reuse a component:** adopt code behind a replaceable interface.
- **Reuse a pattern:** reproduce the idea while changing assumptions or implementation.
- **Study only:** retain as evidence or a test comparator.
- **Reject for this use:** document the mismatch and conditions that could change the decision.

“Build our own” is not the default outcome. It requires the same evidence and a documented reason existing options cannot meet the bounded need.

## 10. Proposed evaluation sequence

### Step 1: one common scenario

Use the same low-risk issue, participants, accessibility requirements, visibility, authority statement, and challenge case across candidate tools. A school attendance policy, neighborhood street redesign, or cooperative equipment budget can work if the actual scenario remains non-binding during research.

### Step 2: four reference runs

1. Run a complete public-participation flow in Decidim or CONSUL.
2. Run a group-decision flow in Loomio or Your Priorities.
3. Run opinion mapping in Polis.
4. Run fiscal follow-through in Open Collective or an administrative sandbox.

Record what must be transferred manually and which meanings are lost.

### Step 3: specialist labs

Test delegation with LiquidFeedback-like rules, verifiability with Helios, policy execution with PolicyKit/Gateway, and configurable authorization with Aragon patterns. Keep these labs separate from real civic authority.

### Step 4: shared-contract prototype

Represent the issue, actors, eligibility, authority, decision, action, record, challenge, and repair through AUU's common contracts while existing tools provide the user-facing functions. Test whether an outsider can reconstruct the complete process.

### Step 5: decide at the smallest scope

Choose adoption, integration, component reuse, pattern reuse, study, or rejection for each requirement. Do not decide that one platform must become the permanent AUU stack merely because it won one pilot.

## 11. AUU's comparative claim

The defensible claim is narrow:

> AUU proposes to connect civic origination, deliberation, bounded authorization, delegation, action, records, challenge, repair, and federation through one explicit model of people, standing, authority, visibility, and recognition.

Based on the first-party material reviewed here, no single compared tool makes that entire combination its documented center. That observation has limits:

- the source review is not an exhaustive product audit;
- features and projects change;
- a tool may support a practice without using AUU's words;
- integration among existing tools may meet the need better than a new platform; and
- AUU currently has a broad specification but no comparable implementation evidence.

AUU is therefore not entitled to say it is more secure, private, democratic, accessible, scalable, or legitimate. It may say that it is investigating a different integration problem and will reuse mature work wherever the evidence supports reuse.
