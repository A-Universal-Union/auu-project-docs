# A Universal Union: Research Index

**Status:** Working evidence register, checked 27 August 2026. This is not a literature review and does not endorse a technology merely because it appears here.

## 1. Purpose

AUU draws on civic practice, institutional design, accessibility, security, privacy, identity, cryptography, deliberation, human-computer interaction, and operations. A long list of links can create the appearance of research without showing what any source actually supports. This index instead records:

- the question a source helps answer;
- its publication and current status;
- the claim AUU may reasonably draw from it;
- what it does **not** establish; and
- the next AUU artifact or test that should use it.

The [research and development strategy](3.research-and-development-strategy.md) governs how evidence becomes a requirement, experiment, pilot, or qualified deployment. The [tool comparison](7.tool-comparison.md) keeps product evidence separate from standards and research evidence.

## 2. Evidence rules

### 2.1 A citation is not inherited assurance

A design does not become secure because it cites NIST, accessible because it cites W3C, or democratic because it cites a civic platform. AUU must identify the exact requirement used, show how the implementation meets it, test the result, and preserve contrary evidence.

### 2.2 Status matters

Every source record should use one of these labels:

- **Final standard or recommendation:** formally published in the named standards process.
- **Draft:** useful for direction or experimentation; not final.
- **Peer-reviewed research:** reviewed scholarly work; still bounded by method and setting.
- **Preprint or working paper:** not yet established through peer review.
- **Maintainer documentation:** first-party description of a product or method.
- **Independent evaluation:** review by a party other than the builder or seller.
- **Field record:** evidence from a real deployment, with known context and limitations.
- **AUU result:** evidence produced by this project, with protocol and conflicts disclosed.
- **Historical or superseded:** retained to understand prior work, not used as the current basis.

The register must also mark a source **current**, **superseded**, **withdrawn**, **contested**, or **unverified** as of its last check.

### 2.3 Claims need boundaries

For each claim, record:

- artifact and version;
- population and setting;
- threat model or comparison condition;
- method and sample;
- result and uncertainty;
- known counterevidence;
- funding and material conflicts;
- reviewer and review date; and
- expiry or event that triggers re-review.

“Worked in a study,” “implemented by a product,” “conforms to a standard,” and “safe for this use” are four different claims.

## 4. Research questions by workstream

### 4.1 Ethics, standing, and institutional power

The central questions are not software questions:

- How can affected people gain standing without having to join the institution that affects them?
- Who governs AUU's rights floor, and what protects pluralism from becoming permission for abuse?
- When do member authorization, legal office, public recognition, and practical control conflict?
- Which forms of challenge and appeal remain credible when the operator, funder, or government is implicated?
- How should burden, benefit, risk, and exclusion be distributed?

This workstream needs comparative constitutional, administrative, cooperative, Indigenous-governance, labor, community-governance, transitional-justice, and institutional-design research. Sources must be selected with scholars and practitioners from the relevant traditions; AUU should not build a universal taxonomy from one country's public law.

**First evidence artifact:** a comparative case matrix that describes the decision, affected groups, source of authority, challenge path, repair, funding, and failure without ranking cultures against a single assumed ideal.

### 4.2 Identity, eligibility, pseudonyms, and recovery

Standards can describe credentials and authenticators. AUU still must research:

- how a process establishes one eligible participant without demanding one permanent public identity;
- what issuers and verifiers learn;
- how contexts remain separated;
- how people without documents, devices, stable addresses, or private spaces participate;
- how recovery avoids both permanent exclusion and silent authority theft;
- how legal demands, compromised endpoints, collusion, timing, and network data defeat a narrow privacy design; and
- how identity requirements alter power between participants, operators, issuers, employers, households, and governments.

**First evidence artifact:** a set of use-specific identity profiles. Each profile states eligibility, minimum disclosure, issuer trust, observer model, recovery, exclusion, appeal, expiry, and prohibited claims.

### 4.3 Deliberation, synthesis, and manipulation

Research should compare structured argument, facilitation, small-group deliberation, large-scale opinion mapping, moderation, translation, ranking, and source-linked synthesis. It must ask:

- Which formats help readers find reasons and evidence without suppressing narrative, emotion, or lived experience?
- Which participants are amplified or exhausted by the format?
- Can a summary preserve disagreement and uncertainty?
- How can a person correct material misrepresentation?
- What distinguishes hidden sponsorship and fabricated grassroots support—**astroturfing**—from legitimate organization?
- Which signals invite gaming once published?
- How do ranking, recommendation, and moderation systems shift agenda power?

Product behavior described in [the tool comparison](7.tool-comparison.md) supplies prototypes and field leads, not proof of deliberative quality.

**First evidence artifact:** a common, consented dataset processed through human facilitation, multiple structured formats, and multiple analytic approaches, followed by source-tracing, correction, privacy, and affected-person review.

### 4.4 Authority, delegation, collectives, and federation

This workstream must test whether people can distinguish:

- preference from authorization;
- role from person;
- office from consent;
- delegation from representation;
- member mandate from external recognition;
- technical connection from federation authority; and
- federation from absorption.

Delegation research must include concentration, cycles, onward delegation, strategic revocation, coercion, low participation, expertise claims, conflicts, and the effect of public delegation records. Federation research must include refused recognition, competing collectives, overlapping jurisdictions, dissent, exit, and failed migration—not only cooperative API exchange.

**First evidence artifact:** tabletop cases where multiple collectives and authorities disagree, followed by participant teach-back of what each claim permits.

### 4.5 Voting, receipts, secrecy, and coercion

AUU should begin with the election or decision context, not a favored protocol. Research must distinguish:

- submission acknowledgment;
- inclusion verification;
- tally or process verification;
- delegation status;
- proof of a participant's choice; and
- public evidence that the decision rule accepted by the named authority was followed.

These proofs create different coercion, sale, retaliation, and association risks. A participant-verifiable artifact is not automatically safe to show to another person. A protocol-level result also does not solve malicious clients, compromised devices, registration, recovery, denial of service, accessibility, procedural authority, or physical coercion.

Helios is a useful laboratory precedent because it offers ballot tracking while its own [FAQ](https://vote.heliosvoting.org/faq) recommends against high-stakes public-office Internet elections. That explicit boundary is part of the evidence, not a footnote to remove.

**First evidence artifact:** a receipt-and-observer matrix tested against low-stakes, organizational, and public-election threat models. Until the desired combination is validated, AUU must label coercion resistance and transferable inclusion evidence as an unresolved tradeoff.

### 4.6 Accessibility, language, and comprehension

Conformance checks must be combined with research involving people who vary in disability, literacy, language, device, connection, time, trust, civic knowledge, and safety. The core question is not whether users can finish a screen. It is whether they can reconstruct consequence, audience, authority, and recourse.

W3C's [reading-level guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reading-level) supports providing a more understandable version when content requires advanced reading ability. It does not justify hiding the exact rule from the simplified path.

**First evidence artifact:** a teach-back protocol for the eight AUU reader questions, with severity-coded misconceptions and results reported by relevant participant group.

### 4.7 Records, harmful material, and public accountability

AUU needs evidence on:

- append-only and correctable records;
- provenance and versioning;
- safe redaction and protected review;
- retention, deletion, legal hold, migration, and shutdown;
- preservation of procedural evidence without redistributing abuse, private data, or illegal material;
- audit access and reviewer accountability; and
- long-term interpretation when software, keys, links, formats, or institutions disappear.

Transparent-log research is relevant to integrity. It is not permission to make every record public or permanent.

**First evidence artifact:** a data and visibility map for one complete civic process, including harmful-evidence cases, access records, correction, export, and end-of-life.

### 4.8 Operations, funding, law, and maintenance

Research must include the institutions around the code:

- hosting and update authority;
- moderation and protected review staffing;
- incident response and participant support;
- procurement, accessibility, child-user, election, privacy, employment, and public-record duties in each deployment;
- funder, vendor, app-store, credential-issuer, and infrastructure-provider leverage;
- security updates, dependency abandonment, and reproducible builds;
- succession, migration, continuity, and shutdown; and
- remedies when a promise cannot be kept.

**First evidence artifact:** an operational assurance case for a deliberately small pilot, including costs, roles, conflicts, failure drills, exit, and participant remedy.

## 5. Source record template

Each entry in the working register should contain:

```text
Source ID:
Title and version:
Author or issuing body:
URL or persistent identifier:
Publication status:
Status checked on:
Question addressed:
Exact claim supported:
Relevant population, setting, and threat model:
Method and evidence:
Important limits:
Counterevidence or dispute:
Funding and conflicts:
AUU artifact affected:
Decision or test triggered:
Reviewer:
Next review event or date:
```

Quotations should be short and used only when exact wording matters. Notes should distinguish the source's words from AUU's interpretation.

## 6. Research review workflow

1. **State the question.** Do not begin with a technology or conclusion.
2. **Search across disciplines and jurisdictions.** Record the search terms, databases, dates, and important exclusions.
3. **Prefer the authoritative artifact.** Use the final standard, current maintainer documentation, original paper, official decision, or complete report when available.
4. **Extract bounded claims.** Record what the source establishes and what it leaves open.
5. **Seek counterevidence.** Include failures, critiques, negative results, and affected-person accounts.
6. **Review with relevant expertise.** Cryptography, accessibility, law, community practice, operations, and institutional design are not interchangeable specialties.
7. **Translate evidence into a requirement or experiment.** A source that changes no decision may not belong in the active register.
8. **Publish the reasoning.** Preserve minority reports and protected review boundaries.
9. **Recheck living sources.** Product documentation, laws, standards drafts, security guidance, and repository status can change.

## 7. Priority evidence gaps

| Priority | Gap | Why it blocks progress |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Governed rights-floor charter and appeal model | The project cannot claim pluralism with protected rights until it states who interprets and changes those rights. |
| 2 | Affected-nonparticipant standing cases | Participation must not become the price of protection or challenge. |
| 3 | Authority and recognition model | Software records cannot answer who may bind whom without this model. |
| 4 | Visibility and harmful-record protocol | “Public by default” can expose people or redistribute harm. |
| 5 | Receipt/coercion evidence | Existing language otherwise promises incompatible voting properties. |
| 6 | Usable recovery and exclusion research | Strong authentication can create silent civic exclusion or authority theft. |
| 7 | Source-linked, correctable synthesis | Automated interpretation may become unaccountable agenda power. |
| 8 | Accessibility and comprehension baseline | A process people cannot understand cannot support informed authority. |
| 9 | Funding, operations, and capture model | Infrastructure owners can acquire undeclared civic power. |
| 10 | Federation conflict and exit cases | Friendly interoperability does not show that independent collectives remain independent. |

## 8. Maintenance

Review this index at each program gate and whenever a cited standard changes status, a serious vulnerability or replication result appears, a product changes license or stewardship, a pilot contradicts a claim, or a jurisdiction changes relevant duties.

The goal is not to accumulate an impressive bibliography. It is to make each consequential AUU claim traceable to current evidence, visible uncertainty, a testable artifact, and a decision that can be challenged.
